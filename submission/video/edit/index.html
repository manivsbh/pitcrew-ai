Heavy machines fail quietly, and the fix is buried in a manual nobody can find.
PitCrew gives the machine a brain.
It starts from a drawing it has never seen, a 1968 public-domain pump patent.
Meta's muse-spark reads the sheet, asks what's missing, and I answer in plain words.
Finalize, and the drawing becomes a 3D machine.
Then Meta's eyes check the work.
SAM 2.1 segments the drawing, muse-spark compares it with the render, moves what's out of place, and writes rules it reuses on every machine.
Every call in the corner is live.
It's a working twin. Assemble it step by step, explode it, run it, isolate any part.
Ask what if. Twenty percent more speed, and the physics sandbox recalculates before anything changes.
On the floor, the engineer just talks.
Meta wav2vec 2.0 transcribes on the device, and the crew answers out loud with Meta's text to speech.
Five seconds of machine sound from the phone.

Outer-ring damage, ninety five percent confidence.

1 masks each part to guide the teardown.
Push back, and it doesn't fold.
